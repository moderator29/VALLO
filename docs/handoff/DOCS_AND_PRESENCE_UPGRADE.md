# DOCS, HELP AND PRESENCE: THE GRADE 1 UPGRADE

The founder's ruling, 9 November 2026. This is a FOLLOW UP to the prompt the
session has already been given, for the SAME session on the SAME branch
`platform-grade-1-readiness`. It does not replace anything. It adds the one
part of the platform that has had the least love and is the most visible to a
stranger deciding whether to trust Vallo with two million naira: the written
surface. The help centre, the legal and policy documents, the guides, the
receipts, the admin desk's own manual, and every place a member reaches them
from.

Read this with `PLATFORM_GRADE_1_READINESS.md` (the rules, how the founder
works) and `FOUNDER_BRIEF_VERBATIM.md` (his own words, the source of truth).
All of those rules apply here without exception.

**The founder is sending more Hauslet screenshots of exactly these screens.**
Study every one before you design. The patterns in section 2 were read off the
batch he has already sent; the ones still coming will add to it, not replace

## 2. WHAT THE HAUSLET SCREENSHOTS SHOW

Read these as patterns to beat, never as assets to copy. Do not take their
lilac and purple, their logo, their wording or their screenshots. Take the
STRUCTURE and raise it into Vallo's dark neon blue and light themes.

### 2.1 A documentation shell of its own
Every documentation page carries a consistent header: the brand glyph, the
section name (`Pages`, `Legal`, `Help Center`), and a search control on the
right. The product chrome falls away. The reader knows they are in the written
part of the platform and can search from anywhere in it.

### 2.2 The legal index is a set of cards, not a list of links
Each document is a card: a rounded tinted square holding a thin line icon, a
bold title, one plain sentence saying what is inside, and a chevron. Above
them, a short paragraph saying what the platform is and what these documents
cover. Below them, a "Still have questions?" panel that leads to support.

The effect is that a nervous reader can scan nine documents in four seconds
and know which one answers their question. A bare list of blue links cannot do
that.

### 2.3 An article opens by telling you what it will say
The pattern, in order: a category pill (icon plus label, tinted), the title, a
"Last updated" date, one intro paragraph that states the scope in plain words,
inline links to the two or three sibling documents a reader might actually
want instead, and then a tinted summary panel of three or four bullets, the
"at a glance" version for someone who will not read the whole thing.

The date matters more than it looks. A policy with a visible recent date reads
as maintained. One with no date reads as abandoned.

### 2.4 A table of contents the reader can use
A card headed TABLE OF CONTENTS with a numbered-list icon, then one tappable
row per section with a chevron, numbered. On a long policy this is the
difference between a document and a wall.

### 2.5 Tables do the heavy lifting
Where a concept has states, fees or rules, it is a table, not prose. Their
payout table is three columns: State, What it means, Withdrawable? Header row
tinted and bold, generous row padding, wraps cleanly at phone width. A member
reads it once and understands the whole money lifecycle.

Ours must go further: this is where the founder's "more rich looks, ours own,
make it advanced" applies most directly. See section 4.3.

### 2.6 Help search is a sheet, with a way in for people who do not know what to search
The search rises from the bottom: a title with a close control, a large
rounded input, then "Popular topics" as chips (Cancellation, Refunds, Caution
fee, Payouts, Verify my ID, List my property), and one cross-link out for
people who are in the wrong place entirely ("Looking for a place? Search
homes"). The chips are the important part: most people cannot name their
problem, but they recognise it.

Results are grouped by kind, and each result shows the title, a grey line
naming the document it sits in, and a snippet with the matched words in bold.

### 2.7 The guides index reads like a magazine, not a FAQ
A title and one-line promise, horizontal category chips with one selected, a
featured card carrying a real product screenshot, a kicker line (FEATURED and
the category), the title, a description, the author and date, then "Latest".
It is designed to be browsed by someone who is not yet in trouble.

### 2.8 The step-by-step guide is the strongest thing they have
"How to list your property" opens with a "Before you start" checklist (what
you need in hand: photos, address, price, a verified ID), then numbered steps.
Each step is a short paragraph followed by **an actual screenshot of that step
in the real product**, complete with its own chrome: the step counter ("Step 5
of 12"), the Back and Continue controls, and the "Saved" mark.

This is why their platform feels established. The guide proves the product
exists and works. Prose alone never does that. **Vallo must do this, which is
why section 5 is about capturing real signed-in screens.**

### 2.9 Where documents are reached from
Their signed-out Profile tab carries two groups: PREFERENCES (currency) and
SUPPORT (Help Center, Guides and articles, Privacy, Legal). Thin icons,
chevrons, nothing else competing. Documentation is one tap from the main tab
bar, for a signed-out visitor, before they have given anything away.

That placement is the answer to the founder's "arranged where to access it
easily inside platform". Ours must be at least as reachable.

## 3. THE VALLO VERSION: WHAT "BETTER" MEANS HERE

Hauslet's documentation is calm, white and well organised. Ours has to be that
AND carry Vallo's identity, which is darker, richer and more alive. Beating
them is not a matter of more words. It is these seven things.

1. **Both themes, properly.** Theirs is light only. Ours is dark neon blue by
   default (page about `#000612`, cards about `#040a20`, raised about
   `#0b1838`, action about `#0066FF`) and light when chosen. Every card, table,
   callout, chip, code block and diagram is designed twice and checked twice.
   A documentation page that looks unfinished in dark mode is unfinished.
2. **Motion that earns its place.** Section rows expand, the table of contents
   marks where you are as you scroll, the search sheet rises, a copied link
   confirms itself, a step advances. All of it brief, all of it respecting
   reduced motion. Nothing decorative that delays a reader.
3. **Richer tables.** See 4.3. Theirs are static. Ours carry state colour,
   icons, and where it helps, a small interactive element.
4. **Real product screenshots, in both themes.** Theirs are light only and
   hand-taken. Ours are captured by a script from a real signed-in session, so
   they can be regenerated when a screen changes instead of rotting.
5. **Tools, not only text.** A help page about fees should let a reader put a
   number in and see what they pay. See 4.6.
6. **The admin and staff desks get documentation too.** Hauslet's is all
   member-facing. Vallo is onboarding support staff, business developers and
   social media managers: they need a manual, and it should be as good as the
   member one.
7. **Honesty as a feature.** Every money page says exactly what is true today,
   including what is NOT live yet. See section 7.

## 4. WHAT TO BUILD

### 4.1 The documentation shell
One frame used by every written page: brand glyph, section name, search
control, and a breadcrumb back into the product. It must work as a page inside
the app shell AND as a standalone page a link can land on, because members
will share these links.

### 4.2 The index card (`DocCard`)
The core component. A rounded tinted container holding a thin line icon, a
title, a one-sentence description, and a chevron. Variants for the legal
index, the help centre, the guides index and the admin manual. The icon
container is a real design element, not a decoration: it is what makes a list
of nine documents scannable. Size, radius, tint and icon weight come from the
Grade 1 design language, and every card in the platform uses the same one.

### 4.3 The rich table (`DocTable`)
Where the founder's "those tables more rich looks, ours own, make it advanced"
lands. Build one table component that handles:
- a tinted, sticky header row;
- a **state column** that carries a coloured dot or pill, not just a word, so
  Pending, Clearing, Available and Paid are distinguishable at a glance;
- a **yes/no column** that uses a mark rather than the words where that is
  clearer;
- an optional leading icon per row;
- correct behaviour at 360px: it does not scroll sideways off the screen, it
  reflows, and it stays readable;
- both themes;
- real semantic table markup, so a screen reader reads it as a table.

### 4.4 The article furniture
- `DocHeader`: category pill, title, last-updated date, intro.
- `DocSummary`: the tinted "at a glance" panel of three or four bullets.
- `DocToc`: the table of contents card, with the current section marked as the
  reader scrolls, and anchors that survive being shared.
- `DocCallout`: note, warning and "this is not live yet" variants.
- `DocCrossLink`: the inline pointer to a sibling document.

### 4.5 The search sheet
Rises from the bottom. Large input. Popular topics as chips, chosen from what
members actually ask (see the support tickets). Results grouped by kind with
the parent document named and the matched words marked. It must work with no
network by falling back to the topic chips rather than showing an error.

### 4.6 The tools
This is what lifts ours above theirs. At least these, each inside the page
that explains the concept:
- **A fee calculator.** A member puts in a rent or a nightly rate and sees
  exactly what they pay or receive, itemised, with the same numbers the real
  checkout would produce. It must read the real fee configuration, never
  hardcoded figures, so it cannot drift from the product.
- **A step guide player.** The numbered steps of a flow with their real
  screenshots, advanced by a control, so a member can walk the whole listing
  wizard before starting it.
- **A copy control** on anything a member may need to quote to support.
- **A "was this helpful"** that writes to the support desk, so the help centre
  tells you which pages are failing.

### 4.7 The guides index
Title, one-line promise, category chips, a featured card with a real
screenshot, kicker, author and date, then the rest. Browsable by someone who
is not yet in trouble.

## 5. SCREENSHOTS OF THE REAL PLATFORM, SIGNED IN

The guides are only as good as the screens in them, and the screens have to be
real.

**The repository already has the harness. Do not write a new one.**
`scripts/marketing/capture/` signs in and captures real routes:
`session.mjs` (sign in, keep the session), `plan.mjs` (the list of captures),
`run.mjs` (the runner), `probe.mjs`, `lock.mjs`, `sheet.mjs`. It writes to
`docs/marketing/source/` with a `capture-report.json` beside the images.
Extend `plan.mjs` with the screens the documentation needs. Your job is to
widen it, not to replace it.

**Credentials.** The founder has two accounts for this, a test account and a
session account. They are supplied at RUN TIME as `QA_MEMBER_EMAIL` and
`QA_MEMBER_PASSWORD`, which is what the harness already reads.

> **NEVER commit, print, paste or log either address or the password.** Not in
> a file, not in a doc, not in a test fixture, not in a commit message, not in
> a chat message, not in a screenshot of a terminal. If you need a second
> account, read it from a second pair of variables. A captured screenshot must
> not show the account's email address: blank it or use a display name.

**What to capture**, both themes, phone and desktop:
- the listing wizard, every step, with its real step counter and Saved mark;
- verification, every step;
- booking, paying and the receipt;
- messages, including voice notes and reactions;
- the host and agent desks;
- Circle and Roommates, the flows a newcomer meets first;
- the admin desks, for the staff manual;
- the empty, loading and error states, which are the ones members photograph
  and send to support.

**Keep them fresh.** A guide with a screenshot of a screen that no longer
exists is worse than no screenshot. Record in the capture report which
document uses which image, and add a check that fails when a document
references an image the plan no longer produces.

## 6. REMOVE WHAT IS NO LONGER USED

> "All of the hold stuffs we not using anymore, eg payluk, delete it wide
> platform, and let make it coordinated."

Payluk alone is about **1,300 references across about 165 files**. This is a
real piece of work and it must be done carefully, because some of those
references MUST NOT be removed.

**Phase 1, audit only, no deletion.** Go through every reference and classify
it into exactly one of five buckets, then show the founder the list before
touching anything:

1. **Dead code**: the adapter, the client, the webhook route, the types, the
   fixtures, the preview fixtures, the vendored source under
   `docs/payments/payluk-source/`. This goes.
2. **Active code to re-point**: the arrangement state machine still speaks
   Payluk's vocabulary, the member screens are Payluk-shaped. This is remapped
   by the protected payments work, not deleted out from under it. **Coordinate
   with that build so the two do not fight over the same files.**
3. **Shared code to keep**: the provider abstraction, the rail router, the
   ledger, the reconciliation cron, the kill switches. These are not Payluk's,
   they are the platform's, and the next provider needs them.
4. **Historical records to leave exactly alone**: rows in `ledger_entries` and
   `provider_arrangements`, and anything in a receipt a member has already
   been shown. You never rewrite what a member was told happened to their
   money.
5. **Applied migrations: NEVER EDIT.** About 15 migration files mention
   Payluk. They are applied history and the migration gate enforces that they
   are never changed. Removing Payluk from the database is a NEW migration
   that drops what is unused, not an edit to an old one. Treat the probes the
   same way: update a probe deliberately, with a dated note saying why.

**Then look wider.** Payluk was named as the example, not the whole job. Also
sweep for: abandoned feature flags, preview fixtures for screens that no
longer exist, dead routes, unused exports (knip already reports these in CI),
retired components, and documentation describing things that were removed. The
goal is the founder's word: **coordinated**. A codebase where everything
present is load bearing.

**The rule throughout: deleting is a change like any other.** Every removal
runs the gates, and a removal that breaks a test means you misclassified
something, not that the test is wrong.

## 7. WHAT THE DOCUMENTS MUST SAY ABOUT MONEY

This is the part where getting it wrong is not a design problem.

- **Protected payment is NOT live.** No document may describe it in the present
  tense. Where a page needs to mention it, it says what is true: card payment
  through our payment partner today, protected payment coming.
- **Vallo never holds member money.** The repository enforces this wording
  (`check-valuation-words.mjs` and the custody tests). Do not work around the
  gate; if the gate refuses your sentence, the sentence is wrong.
- **When the protected rail does go live**, the documents must name the
  provider and the custodian bank, because that is a condition of the
  agreement: see `PROTECTED_PAYMENTS_BUILD.md` section 3 and the appendix in
  `PROTECTED_PAYMENTS_SPECS.md`. The funds wording is fixed text and must
  appear unaltered.
- **Fees shown in a document come from the same configuration the checkout
  uses.** Never a number typed into a paragraph.
- **Rent paid from outside Nigeria** has a rule, in section 6 of the payments
  build document. The documents must state it plainly rather than let a
  diaspora member discover it at the payment step.

The money-copy sweep already in the session's prompt feeds straight into this:
whatever that sweep finds, the corrected wording lands here.

## 8. WHERE MEMBERS REACH ALL OF THIS

Writing it is half the work. The founder's actual complaint is that it is not
**arranged** and not **reachable**. Build the routes in, deliberately:

- **A documentation home**, with the three sections (help, guides, legal) and
  the search.
- **From the profile or account tab, signed in AND signed out**, in a clearly
  labelled support group, the way the reference does it. A signed-out visitor
  must be able to read every policy before creating an account.
- **In context, at the moment of doubt.** A link to the fee explanation beside
  the fee. A link to the cancellation policy on the booking screen. A link to
  the verification guide inside the verification stepper. A link to the payout
  explanation on the payout screen. This is worth more than the whole index
  page, because it catches the member where the question actually forms.
- **From the AI assistant**, which should be able to answer from these
  documents and link to them, with the button through to a human.
- **From the empty and error states**, which are where confused members are.
- **From receipts and emails**, which are read outside the app.
- **From the admin desks** to the staff manual.

Every document gets a stable, shareable URL that survives the page being
redesigned.

## 10. THE ADMIN AND STAFF MANUAL

Hauslet's documentation is entirely member-facing. Vallo is onboarding support
staff, business developers and social media managers who have never seen the
desks. They need:

- what each desk is for, and who gets access;
- how to work a queue: listings to approve, identity checks, reports, support
  tickets, refunds;
- the decisions a staff member may and may not take alone;
- what is logged, and what a member can see of a staff action;
- the escalation path, and who to reach out of hours;
- the same quality bar as the member documentation: cards, tables, real
  screenshots, both themes.

Same shell, same components, behind the staff gate.
